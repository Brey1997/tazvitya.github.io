09

Machine learning · R · Productivity analytics

Garment factory productivity modeling

A predictive analysis of garment-worker productivity that compares statistical and regularized modeling approaches for sewing and finishing departments.

Project approach

The workflow cleans department and calendar fields, imputes missing values with bagged trees, examines distributions and correlations, and estimates department-specific OLS models. It tests multicollinearity, heteroskedasticity, and autocorrelation, then compares OLS, WLS, Ridge, LASSO, and Elastic Net using cross-validation.

Selected findings

For the sewing data, OLS produced the strongest reported cross-validated R² (0.584), while Elastic Net produced the lowest reported MAE (0.1002). Operational factors—including targeted productivity, idle staffing, style changes, task complexity, workforce size, and work in progress—were examined as potential productivity drivers.

15

R · Tidyverse · Data visualization

Real or fake job postings

An exploratory visual analysis of 17,880 job listings that compares legitimate and fraudulent postings across employment type, salary, experience requirements, telecommuting, and company-logo availability.

Project approach

The reproducible Quarto workflow cleans the source data, separates and standardizes salary ranges, and builds two univariate plots, one bivariate plot, one trivariate plot, one quadrivariate plot, and two descriptive tables. The final report applies clear presentation principles so both technical and nontechnical readers can interpret the results.

Selected findings

Full-time roles account for nearly 65% of the listings. Salary values are strongly right-skewed, full-time roles show the widest salary spread, and fraudulent postings appear more often among telecommuting listings without a company logo. Fraudulent listings also concentrate in entry-level, full-time roles. The dataset contains 17,014 legitimate and 866 fraudulent postings.
